I used to wait until ideas felt “finished” before sharing. The result was fewer artifacts and slower learning. Publishing early drafts changed that.

- **Transparency builds trust.** Students appreciate seeing the messy intermediate steps and how I debug models in real time.
- **Repetition beats novelty.** Revisiting the same idea through posts, talks, and code samples helps it stick for both me and the audience.
- **Small wins compound.** A simple notebook that solves one thorny problem often becomes the most reused resource in a workshop.
- **Feedback comes faster.** Early readers highlight missing context, shaky assumptions, or better baselines long before a launch.

If you’re teaching ML, consider sharing the journey instead of just the destination. The community benefits, and you will too.
